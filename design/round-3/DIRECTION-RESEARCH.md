## Owner correction and direction reset — 2026-08-03

This section supersedes the earlier ranking and direction conclusions retained below as
historical evidence. The Fold is rejected and is not eligible for refinement or mobile
adaptation. Product Theatre contributes information discipline only; Project Panorama is
the visual foundation only. Neither previous composition is owner-approved.

**The Project Edit** is the sole provisional direction: one compact, functional campaign-
level panorama creates project context, followed immediately by four equally weighted,
generously sized product columns that continue vertically. The layout must feel like a
premium home-improvement editorial joined directly to a practical retail shelf—not a hero
followed by generic cards.

Its signature, the **Ekoway Project Cue**, combines the campaign headline, selected category,
real category teaser, and the only category controls. A restrained orange diagonal cut and
deep-green readiness line carry a thin category accent into the card edge. Product cards use
Product Theatre's disciplined name, category, price, stock, opening, and secondary-cart
hierarchy, while omitting descriptions, ratings, badges, and unsupported specifications.

The corrected desktop review framework is: visual attractiveness/first-screen impact 20%,
product presentation/emotional appeal 20%, premium/professional quality 20%, modern commercial
quality 15%, clarity/comparison 10%, innovation/signature 10%, and mobile potential 5%.
Visual attractiveness, product presentation, premium quality, professional quality, and
innovation must each score at least 4/5. Production-readiness scoring is deferred.

## 1. Principles worth adopting

- [Festool](https://www.festoolusa.com/): express authority through application knowledge,
  systematic facts and service confidence—not dense technical chrome.
- [Bang & Olufsen](https://www.bang-olufsen.com/en/us): use proportion, restraint and one
  confident product moment, while keeping real products in the first viewport.
- [Material Bank](https://www.materialbank.com/): make material tactility support fast,
  familiar search and filtering; avoid boards, enterprise workflow and sample metaphors that
  look like software.
- [Schoolhouse](https://schoolhouse.com/): combine utility, warmth, character and a sense of
  home so practical products can still carry emotional value.
- [Rejuvenation](https://www.rejuvenation.com/): connect design inspiration to useful
  categories and guidance without separating commerce from the story.
- [Dyson](https://www.dyson.com/): spotlight product confidence and legible facts; avoid
  photography-dependent emptiness.
- [Ferguson Home](https://www.build.com/showroom/branch/new-brunswick-nj-showroom-3769):
  balance homeowner aspiration with professional expertise and project usefulness.

## 2. Patterns that created the rejected directions

- Product Theatre reduced innovation to a large feature tile plus ordinary supporting cards.
- Technical Atelier used dark modules, technical labels and dashboard-like repetition.
- Pass 2 treated products as comparison rows instead of desirable retail objects.
- A hero/category/grid sequence separates emotion from the buying decision and becomes
  generic once the logo changes.
- Decorative colour, texture, radii or motion cannot rescue an unchanged composition.

## 3. Opportunities specific to Ekoway

- Treat the hardware counter as an editorial place where project ambition and exact stock
  truth meet, rather than copying a national big-box catalogue.
- Make the absence of product photography feel curated and transparent through material
  fields, physical labels and deliberate image-status copy.
- Use connected browsing structures that keep several products comparable while one gains
  emotional emphasis.
- Let the logo anchor recognition while a distinctive composition—not green/orange alone—
  creates memory.

## 4. Premium missing-image principles

- Separate category atmosphere from the product-image status with explicit labels.
- Reserve stable image space and use layered paper, material and print-detail cues rather
  than empty rectangles, initials or fake silhouettes.
- Keep product facts outside the atmospheric field so no backdrop implies an exact product.
- Use the same honest phrase everywhere: “Exact product image unavailable.”

## 5. Homeowners versus contractors

- Homeowners need warmth, project context, confident colour and a visible path into products.
- Contractors need fast search, exact prices, stock, aligned facts and predictable actions.
- Both audiences benefit when category context and the decision facts remain in the same
  viewport; neither needs descriptions or unsupported specifications in listings.

## 6. Assumptions

- `catalogue.json` is the complete concept source; no API or backend session is required.
- Name sorting is a valid existing sort and deliberately exposes the longest-name and
  low-stock cases in the review viewport.
- CSS material fields are category atmosphere, not product imagery.
- Add to Cart is visual and side-effect-free; product opening uses a local concept link.
- Mobile strategies are documented now but not rendered until a desktop direction is chosen.

## 7. Creative territory and direction theses

### Direction 1 — Project Panorama

1. **Thesis:** five real products inhabit one continuous renovation panorama, joining
   emotional category context to a shared buying-fact baseline without a hero or card grid.
2. **Audience:** project atmosphere attracts homeowners; aligned facts serve professionals.
3. **Signature:** **Ekoway Panorama** lets focus or selection bring one connected department
   scene forward while neighboring product facts remain comparable.
4. **Premium evidence:** controlled editorial scale, layered material fields, precise labels.
5. **Professional evidence:** exact name, category, price and stock share a stable baseline.
6. **Innovative evidence:** product scenes form one connected landscape, not separate cards.
7. **Emotion:** colour and material rhythm suggest renovation energy without fake products.
8. **First viewport:** compact header, magazine category index and five staggered scenes.
9. **Category logic:** one visible index filters the panorama; search and sort stay familiar.
10. **Missing image:** category atmosphere plus a separate exact-image-unavailable seal.
11. **Comparison:** five fact blocks align at the same lower edge despite staggered scenes.
12. **Colour:** plaster, ink, oxide, workwear blue and moss balance warmth with credibility.
13. **Type:** editorial serif for campaign voice; system sans for commerce facts.
14. **Mobile:** one swipeable scene, next-scene cue and sticky decision baseline.
15. **Commercial risk:** horizontal continuation could be missed.
16. **Implementation risk:** scene expansion must not create overflow or clipped facts.

### Direction 2 — The Fold

1. **Thesis:** four editorial folds keep neighboring products comparable while one opens into
   a richer shopping moment.
2. **Audience:** a welcoming magazine rhythm serves homeowners; retained row facts serve pros.
3. **Signature:** **Ekoway Fold** expands one product in place without hiding adjacent facts.
4. **Premium evidence:** generous light surfaces, tactile sample envelope, composed typography.
5. **Professional evidence:** every fold retains exact price, stock, category and actions.
6. **Innovative evidence:** disclosure changes information rhythm without obscuring shopping.
7. **Emotion:** broad colour fields make products feel curated rather than catalogued.
8. **First viewport:** compact header and controls followed by one open and three closed folds.
9. **Category logic:** one conventional department selector filters the folds.
10. **Missing image:** active sample envelope plus concise image-status labels on closed folds.
11. **Comparison:** neighboring names, prices and stock remain visible during expansion.
12. **Colour:** chalk, aubergine, spruce, terracotta and mineral accents; never full-dark.
13. **Type:** characterful slab/serif display with controlled system-sans facts.
14. **Mobile:** accessible one-open accordion with adjacent summaries retained.
15. **Commercial risk:** closed folds may feel less immediately shoppable than open products.
16. **Implementation risk:** horizontal bands could drift toward the rejected table.
